Top Hits

Jensen Huang leaves Nvidia; the board won't take him back, and Q3 beats anyway — 9

Huang resigned as CEO last week after 33 years, days after admitting an affair and amid reports linking him to a woman alleged to have contact with Chinese intelligence; the board is still investigating, and chair Harvey Jones said Heidrick & Struggles will lead the CEO search. On the Nov 17 earnings call, chairman Harvey Jones said the board is not considering bringing him back, with CFO Colette Kress as interim CEO. The numbers held: Q3 FY2027 revenue rose 103% to $115.8B, data-center revenue rose 110% to $107.6B, and Nvidia guided Q4 to $130B ±3% assuming no China data-center compute. Kress raised next fiscal year's growth outlook to 75%, per CNBC. Demand is intact; the risk has moved to governance and export controls at the company that sets the AI hardware roadmap.

CNBC (board) · NVIDIA (Q3) · CNBC (call)

Experts affirm an AI-generated proof of Yang-Mills existence — 9

OpenAI asked a committee led by theoretical physicist David Gross to vet a proof, generated by its models, of the existence of quantum Yang-Mills theory, a foundation of the Standard Model and one of the Millennium Prize problems. On Nov 10 the committee posted a document explaining the proof and affirming its formal verification in Lean. Scientific American notes the proof is long and messy, and the reviewers were appointed by OpenAI, so journal peer review is still ahead. Separately, physicist Nathaniel Craig says GPT-6 Astra turned his idea into a candidate lattice definition of the Standard Model and led the detailed proof, which lattice theorists reviewed. Machine-checked proofs make AI science claims auditable, which is the part that changes how fast they get accepted.

Scientific American · The Decoder

OpenAI ships GPT-6.2 Astra and Sol at lower cost per task — 8

Nov 17: GPT-6.2 Astra (frontier) and GPT-6.2 Sol (everyday work) launched in ChatGPT Work, Codex and the API. Astra lists at $8 / $40 per million input/output tokens after a 20% cut, with cached input 60% cheaper at $0.40; Sol stays at $2 / $10. OpenAI calls Astra its most capable cyber model to date and launched both with tighter safeguards on higher-risk security help, with reduced-refusal access planned for vetted defenders through Daybreak. The Verge highlights better slides, documents and 3D web coding. Price per finished task, not per token, is now the main competitive lever.

OpenAI · The Verge

Starship's upper stage is caught by the tower after a day in orbit — 8

Flight 15 (Nov 13–14): Starship reached orbit, deployed 26 Starlink V3 satellites, circled Earth 14 times and returned to Starbase about 22.5 hours later, where the tower arms caught it for the first time. The Super Heavy booster ended its boostback early on a low-fuel reading, aborted its own catch, diverted offshore and was destroyed by its flight termination system; SpaceX is investigating. One booster catch away from a fully reusable orbital system, which is what drives launch cost toward Starlink-scale economics.

SpaceX · Aviation Week

Java4Shell: critical remote code execution in Java's Base64 decoder — 8

CVE-2026-114938, disclosed by Calif on Nov 17, is a memory-corruption bug in Java's hardware-accelerated Base64 decoding on x86-64 CPUs with AVX-512. It affects OpenJDK 17.0.6 and later and builds based on it (Oracle, Corretto, Temurin, Microsoft, Zulu, Red Hat). The researchers achieved unauthenticated RCE in default installs of Keycloak, Jira, Confluence, Artifactory, Jenkins and PingFederate; they published a crash proof of concept but not the weaponized exploits. CISA urges immediate patching. Control: apply Oracle's Nov 17 Critical Patch Update, including Java runtimes bundled in desktop and trading apps; until then, start the JVM with -XX:-UseBASE64Intrinsics (or -XX:UseAVX=2).

BleepingComputer · CISA

4,837 hijacked GitHub accounts plant a CI secret stealer — 7

Starting Nov 10, an attacker used stolen GitHub accounts to open test pull requests titled "docs: dependency audit notes" in 61,764 repositories and then pushed a stealer into workflows or npm postinstall hooks. It takes Actions secrets, GITHUB_TOKEN, OIDC tokens, AI-service keys and package, SSH and cloud credentials; SafeDep counted 8,093 repositories with malicious commits. Control: enforce 2FA, review personal access tokens, OAuth apps and SSH keys, require review on workflow changes, and rotate every repo secret if anything unexpected appears.

The Hacker News

Optimizely supply-chain attack serves malware on major sites — 7

Nov 16: malicious JavaScript loaded through Optimizely, an experimentation platform, appeared on sites including Zoom, CNN, Fox News, BBC, Salesforce and GitLab. The scripts tried to steal crypto and payment details and exploit iOS devices. Control: keep browsers and iOS updated, refuse unexpected downloads or "update" prompts even on trusted sites, and site owners should pin third-party scripts with Subresource Integrity or a strict Content Security Policy.

BleepingComputer

AI-agent crews hunt LiteLLM and Dify gateways for API keys — 7

Amazon's MadPot decoys, posing as popular LLM services, captured agent records from Oct 3 showing campaigns that split goals across worker agents and went after AI gateways with default admin credentials, such as LiteLLM's default master key, plus known Langflow and RAGFlow flaws. Amazon assesses with moderate confidence that the operators were separate China-linked groups. Control: never expose AI gateways to the internet, change default keys, and store provider keys as scoped, rotatable secrets.

NoisyTCP: an 11-year-old Linux kernel bug crashes servers — 5

A kernel flaw affecting almost all Linux distributions lets remote attackers crash systems running Nginx, Apache or SSH in default configurations. It causes outages, not data theft. Control: apply your distribution's kernel update when it ships, and keep rate limiting in front of public services.

Quant and algo trading

Fed's Barr: same-model AI trading risks herding — 6

In a Nov 16 speech, Governor Barr said that if traders rely on a small set of similar AI models trained on similar data, their strategies and reactions to news could synchronize, and that self-learning algorithms could learn to manipulate markets, for example through spoofing. For systematic books, model and data diversity, plus kill switches and human accountability, are the controls regulators will look for.
