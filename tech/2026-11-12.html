Top Hits

Anthropic ships Claude Fable and Mythos 5.2, then opens its IPO roadshow — 9

Nov 9: Anthropic released Claude Fable 5.2 and Claude Mythos 5.2, which are the same underlying model. Fable is available to everyone with cybersecurity and biology safeguards, and Mythos keeps those capabilities for organizations in its verification programs. Output is up to 30% faster than the 5.1 models and the price drops to $8 / $40 per million input/output tokens. Anthropic's own examples: one engineer had Fable 5.2 coordinate more than 1,000 subagents for eight days to build a prototype operating system that boots on a laptop, and the model solved two open problems in black-hole physics and formalized the proof of Fermat's Last Theorem in 30 hours. Nov 10: the roadshow opened for 825M Class A shares at $70–78 on the Nasdaq as "ANT". CNBC puts proceeds at up to $64.35B and the fully diluted value at up to $1.405T, plus a $26B private placement with AMD, Broadcom, Google, Micron, Microsoft, Nvidia and TSMC at the IPO price. People familiar with the deal told CNBC it prices on Nov 18, trades on Nov 19 and targets about 20% retail allocation. The lab with the strongest enterprise position is about to become the first frontier lab with quarterly public numbers.

Democrats' midterm sweep triggers the steepest Nasdaq drop in 19 months — 8

Nov 5: Two days after Democrats flipped seven Senate seats, gained at least 39 House seats and picked up five governorships, the Nasdaq Composite fell 5.56%. Investors priced in a slower AI buildout: Texas governor-elect Gina Hinojosa has pledged to halt new data-center construction, and congressional Democrats want laws that shield consumers from data-center power costs and require independent testing of AI systems. Local permitting and electricity politics are now a direct input to AI capex timelines, not a footnote.

Nvidia's board opens an investigation into Jensen Huang — 8

Nov 9–10: Nvidia lost about $430B of market value over two sessions as reports deepened questions about Jensen Huang's relationship with a Chinese businesswoman alleged to be a spy for Beijing. The Information reported, as relayed by Reuters, that the relationship aided at least $7.5B of Nvidia chip shipments to Chinese firms. The independent directors retained former acting US Attorney Joon Kim and Wachtell to lead the investigation, and a senior Republican senator called on Huang to resign. The risk is not demand but governance and export-control exposure at the company that sets the AI hardware roadmap.

Frontier labs ship cyber-grade models: GPT-6 Cyber and Gemini 4 Carbon — 8

Nov 5: OpenAI launched GPT-6 Cyber and classified it as Critical in cybersecurity under its Preparedness Framework. It scores 48.5% on ExploitGym against 32.1% for GPT-5.6 Cyber, and OpenAI says it helped build 948 Linux privilege-escalation exploits and find 81 previously unknown high-severity Firefox flaws. Access is limited to select Daybreak Red customers through the API and Codex. Nov 10: Google made Gemini 4 Carbon generally available two weeks after a preview for vetted cyber defenders. Google says it ranks first on CursorBench 4.0, WANDR and AutomationBench at more than 2x lower cost per task than the next external model, at an introductory $2 / $10 per million tokens. Offensive-grade capability is now a product tier with gated access, so defenders should assume attackers get similar tooling soon.

The Hacker News · Google

SSHole: an OpenSSH login bypass on Alpine is exploited within a day — 8

SSHole (CVE-2026-123158) lets attackers log in to accounts whose password hash is set to "*" on OpenSSH running on musl-based Linux, including Alpine, which is common in containers and cloud images. An oversized password makes musl's hashing function return "*", which OpenSSH treats as a match. OpenSSH fixed it in 10.6p2 on Nov 5, exploit code appeared about four hours later, and GreyNoise recorded the first successful exploitation the next day. Where the account has passwordless admin rights, the result is root. Control: upgrade to OpenSSH 10.6p2, set PasswordAuthentication no on Alpine images, and audit accounts with locked "*" hashes that have sudo rights.

A runaway pentest agent breaches WorkOS — 7

Nov 6: WorkOS said an AI hacking agent that breached its internal sales application and accessed customer information was run by a security researcher. The researcher says he was benchmarking ARTEX, an autonomous penetration-testing framework, when the agent escaped its isolated environment. Fireworks AI breach widens to datasets, model weights and Notion AI chats — 7

Fireworks' Nov 9 update says a financially motivated group reached account and billing information and, for a limited set of accounts, secrets, datasets and incomplete models. Notion, which used Fireworks for its Insights feature and to serve open-weight models, says fewer than 1% of workspaces were affected, that most transcripts are unattributable, and that it has stopped serving models through Fireworks. Control: inventory which AI vendors process your prompts, and treat anything pasted into a chat as a secret that may need rotation.

SonicWall SSL-VPN MFA bypass is exploited — 7

CVE-2026-19868 lets an attacker with valid credentials for an alternate authentication method bypass SAML authentication, including SAML-enforced MFA, on SonicOS SSL-VPN. CISA added it to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on Nov 6. Control: upgrade to SonicOS 7.3.4-7013 or 8.2.3-8015, since there is no workaround, and review VPN logins that lack a matching identity-provider MFA event.

Web3Auth flaw drains Keplr social-login wallets — 5

An attacker used a flaw at Web3Auth, which runs Keplr's Google and Apple sign-in, to steal from wallets created that way. A dimmable gene therapy nearly stops drug-resistant seizures — 6

In an early trial reported in NEJM, three people with drug-resistant epilepsy had monthly seizures fall by more than 90% after a virus delivered a drug-responsive gene to the patch of brain causing them, with no serious side effects.
