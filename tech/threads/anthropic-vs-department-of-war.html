Anthropic vs. Department of War supply-chain-risk case

Running page. Updated weekly by Sun.

Scope

Anthropic PBC v. U.S. Department of War: the Department's designation of Anthropic as a national-security supply-chain risk under the Federal Acquisition Supply Chain Security Act of 2018, after Anthropic refused to remove contract prohibitions on using Claude for lethal autonomous warfare and mass surveillance of Americans; the D.C. Circuit litigation; and what the outcome means for AI vendors' usage limits in defense contracts. Key dates so far: Feb 27, 2026 federal stop-use order and designation announced; Mar 3 formal determination; Mar 9 petition for review; Sep 25 divided 2–1 panel ruling upholding the designation (Judge Henderson dissenting). Running more than 4 weeks, so it has its own page under the standing rule. Linked from Weekly Tech Updates. Unscored; not investment or legal advice.

Weekly log

  • Oct 17, 2026 (week of Oct 10–17) — Weekly Tech Updates · 2026-10-17
    • Oct 13: Anthropic petitioned the D.C. Circuit for panel rehearing or rehearing en banc of the Sep 25 decision; the clerk had withheld issuance of the mandate the same day as the ruling (Defense, 6). Unite.AI
    • The petition argues the majority read "otherwise manipulate" with no subversive-intent requirement, leaving no limiting principle for any contractor, and that skipping pre-designation notice cannot be excused as harmless error (citing Fischer v. United States, Michigan v. EPA and Trump v. Cook). Unite.AI
    • So what: the rehearing decision will set whether an AI vendor can keep usage limits (autonomous weapons, mass surveillance) in defense contracts without risking a supply-chain-risk designation. Watch for a D.C. Circuit order on the petition.
