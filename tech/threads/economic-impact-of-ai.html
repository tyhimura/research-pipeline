Economic Impact of AI (running)

Running page. Updated weekly by Sun.

Scope

Labor and jobs data; productivity studies; AI capex and the debt financing behind it; energy and power demand; GDP and macro effects; policy responses (tax, UBI, etc.); and research on which jobs current models can actually do. Standing thread, exempt from the 4-week graduation rule. Unscored. Not investment advice.

Weekly log

  • Oct 17, 2026 (week of Oct 10–17) — Weekly Tech Updates · 2026-10-17
    • IMF Global Financial Stability Report (Oct 13): hyperscalers now account for 10% of global investment-grade bond issuance, up from under 1% in 2024; an AI correction "will affect the real economy". Reuters via AOL
    • Chicago Fed's Goolsbee (Oct 13): inflation is "starting to feel like aggregate overheating", with AI data-center cost increases "jumping out of its lane" into wider prices. Reuters via Yahoo Finance

    • Pointer: an AI-stock sell-off sent Korea's Kospi down 7.6% on Oct 15 (39% below its June record), while CoreWeave fell 15.5% on Oct 14 and then closed 5.9% higher — AI-capex repricing, Markets (7); Anthropic Q3 also in Markets (7). AP via Click2Houston, "Wall Street muscles higher despite more swings for AI stocks"
    • So what: the macro debate has flipped from "AI boosts growth" to "AI capex as a source of inflation and leverage risk". White-collar cuts tied to AI are now arriving at mainstream employers, not just tech.
  • Oct 10, 2026 (week of Oct 3–10) — Weekly Tech Updates · 2026-10-10

    • Labor (Schwab, Oct 8): no broad AI-driven disruption yet, but job losses concentrated in AI-exposed industries (information, finance); AI investment lifting productivity and profits more than payrolls; import-heavy capex may limit near-term U.S. growth and jobs benefit. Advisor Perspectives

    • Demand signal: Samsung Q3 guidance ~KRW 107.4T operating profit on HBM/DRAM. Samsung
    • So what: the build-out shows up in supplier profits and power contracts before payrolls; labor effects remain sector-concentrated.
