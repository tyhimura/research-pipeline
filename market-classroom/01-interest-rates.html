Interest rates: the price of money

What $100,000 earns in a year

Interest for one year at each rate, from April's Oct 24 wrap

  • What it showsAn interest rate is the yearly rent paid to borrow money. The same $100k earns very different amounts depending on who borrows it and for how long, and gold earns nothing.
  • ExamplesAt 4.999%, $100k lent to the US government for 10 years earns about $4,999 a year. If the 10-year rose to 6% (hypothetical), it would earn $6,000, and holding gold would mean giving up more income.
  • Memory toolRate × $100k = rent. Move the decimal: 4.999% of $100k is $4,999.

How the rent on money flows

Who pays whom, and who sets which rate

  • What it showsThe lender gives up using the cash and takes the risk of not being repaid. The interest rate is the price charged for both.
  • ExamplesA bank borrowing overnight pays near the Fed's 3.75–4.00%. The US government borrowing for 10 years pays 4.999%. A riskier borrower pays more.
  • Memory toolTime + risk = rent. Longer and riskier loans cost more.

Data: April's weekly wrap, Oct 24, 2026. 10-year yield at Friday Oct 23 close (CNBC); Fed target range 3.75–4.00% (Newsquawk).
