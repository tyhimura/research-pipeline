United States

S&P 500

−0.5% week

Level 7,623.68
Sat Oct 24, 03:00 BKK

The S&P 500 slipped 0.5% to 7,623.68, its second straight weekly loss, while the Dow rose 0.4% for a third weekly gain and the Nasdaq lost 0.7%. Europe

STOXX Europe 600

−1.4% week

Level 635.77
Fri Oct 23, 22:30 BKK

The STOXX 600 fell 1.39% to 635.77, its steepest weekly loss since early September, which ended a two-week winning streak. Unrest in France and higher oil dented earnings optimism as SAP, Barclays and Kering reported, and the euro slipped on French budget worries as 70,000 police deployed for Saturday's protests. The index is 3.75% below its August record but still up 7.36% this year. In our deep dive the cheapest European names were cheap for a reason: TF1 and Betsson saw earnings fall in 2026, and Henkel's apparent discount came from the screen counting only its preferred shares. So there is no European pick.

Saudi Arabia

Tadawul All Share (TASI)

−4.1% week

Level 9,695.86
Thu Oct 22, 19:00 BKK

Saudi Arabia's TASI fell 4.06% from Thursday to Thursday to 9,695.86, the biggest drop among our ten markets. It was the eighth weekly fall in a row, the longest losing streak since 1994, and Thursday's 2.3% slide left the index at its lowest close since 2021. Breadth was dire, with 15 gainers against 269 losers on Thursday, and Saudi National Bank and Maaden each fell 5%. Houthi attacks inside Saudi Arabia lifted Brent to its highest since late April, but that did not help shares. Even after the fall the market trades at about 17 times trailing earnings excluding Aramco, and our closest name was only about 11% below value.

Thailand had a four-session week, closed on Friday for Chulalongkorn Day, and the SET Index fell 0.66% from Thursday to Thursday to 1,562.67. Carried over from last week, and cheaper now. Kia fell 3% to KRW 106,900 with no new filing, so the gap to our KRW 162,600 value widened from 32% to 34%. It still clears every bar on its five-year record: about 14% return on capital, KRW 21.6tn of net cash against a KRW 41.5tn market value, and an EV/EBIT near 2.4x on the last twelve months' profit. The value-up plan returns cash through buybacks and dividends. The risks are unchanged and real: tariffs, Chinese competition, a strong won and chaebol cross-holdings. That is why it is rated risk 4. Q3 results on Oct 30 are the next test.

Historical comparison

Kia trades at about 5.8 times trailing earnings. Over the last ten year-ends (2016-2025) its P/E ranged from 3.78 in 2024 to 13.7 in 2020, when earnings slumped, with a median of 6.07. So Kia is not cheap against its own history: today's multiple sits just below its ten-year median, because Korean carmakers have nearly always traded at low multiples. The last time the P/E sat near 3.8-3.9, from end-2022 to end-2024, the share price still rose about 70%, from 59,300 to 100,700 won, because earnings grew, not because the multiple rose. Our case rests on net cash and cash flow, not on the multiple reverting.

Reference: Kia P/E history (companiesmarketcap); closes from Yahoo Finance 000270.KS

Sell when: Reaches ~KRW 162,600 value, or operating margin stays below 5% for four straight quarters, or Kia's Hyundai Mobis stake is sold to the controlling family below market

Governance note: Chaebol circular cross-holding: Hyundai Mobis owns Hyundai Motor, which owns 35.17% of Kia (related parties 36.99%), and Kia holds 18.10% of Mobis, a likely piece in any succession restructuring. Related-party transactions with Mobis were KRW 9.4tn in 2025. Positives: cumulative voting adopted Sep 2026, NPS 7.25%, foreigners 40%. No new governance filing since 18 Oct.
